How does [`gspice.fit()`](/GSPICE/reference/api/#gspice.fit) estimate the mean and the
covariance? It removes outliers from the
[training spectra](/GSPICE/concepts/glossary/#term-training-spectra) in passes, then
estimates both in the [working units](/GSPICE/concepts/glossary/#term-working-units) of
[The model](/GSPICE/concepts/model/#working-units). Five kinds of model share this
function: the default, constant centre, S/N bins, low rank and noise-aware.

## Training spectra

A covariance of npix pixels has npix² entries, so it needs many spectra. `fit` skips a
spectrum with more than `maxbadpix` [bad pixels](/GSPICE/concepts/glossary/#term-bad-pixel)
(64 by default) or with the weight 0. It also skips a spectrum whose pixels a clipping
pass masks completely. $N_s$ counts the spectra that a fit
uses.

With $y_s = a_s \tilde f_s$ the scaled spectrum of [The model](/GSPICE/concepts/model/),
the model of S/N bin $b$ holds

$$
m_b = \frac{1}{N_s} \sum_s y_s, \qquad
C_b = \frac{1}{N_s - 1} \sum_s (y_s - m_b)(y_s - m_b)^\mathsf{T} ,
$$

over the spectra $s$ of the bin. With weights $w_s$, both sums are weighted, and $C_b$
divides by $\sum_s w_s - \sum_s w_s^2 / \sum_s w_s$ instead of $N_s - 1$. The weights
apply to the final fit only, and the clipping passes use unit weights.

A default model needs $N_s - 1 \ge \mathrm{npix}$, and a good pixel in at least one
spectrum at every pixel. Close to that minimum, $C_b$ can still fail the
[condition check](/GSPICE/concepts/numerics/#condition-checks) of its precision matrix.
[What you get back](/GSPICE/start/results/#model) lists the fields of the model.

## Outlier clipping

An artifact in a training spectrum adds a large deviation to the sums above, and so
widens the covariance at its pixels. `fit` therefore flags the
[outliers](/GSPICE/concepts/glossary/#term-outlier) of the training spectra in
[clipping passes](/GSPICE/concepts/glossary/#term-clipping-pass), with thresholds that
fall from pass to pass (paper Alg. 4). The figure follows one spectrum with a bad column
through the three default passes:

<ClippingFigure />

Pass $n$ uses the threshold `nsigma[n - 1]`, from `nsigma=(20, 8, 6)` by default, and the
mask $B_n$, with $B_1$ the bad pixels. It runs four steps:

1. Interpolate the pixels of $B_n$, and scale each spectrum by $a_s$ over its pixels
   outside $B_n$.
2. Fit the covariance, with unit weights.
3. Compute the pixelwise z-score of every pixel of these spectra, interpolated pixels
   included, with `nguard`.
4. Flag $F_n$: each pixel with $|z|$ above the threshold, and its two neighbours.

With `cumulative=True`, the default, $B_{n+1} = B_n \cup F_n$. The final covariance is
fitted on the last of these masks, with the weights. The returned mask is that final
mask, and True at every pixel of a spectrum that `fit` did not use.
`cumulative=False` uses the 2022 rule, $B_{n+1} = \mathrm{bad} \cup F_n$, with the final
mask $\mathrm{bad} \cup F_{N-1} \cup F_N$ for $N$ passes. `nsigma=()` fits without
clipping.

### The clipping ceiling

A pass can flag only the outliers that its z-scores can reach. These z-scores are
[in-sample](/GSPICE/concepts/glossary/#term-in-sample): each spectrum helped to fit the
covariance that predicts it, so an outlier widens the covariance that judges it. For a
default model, the in-sample $|z|$ of an outlier of any amplitude approaches the
[clipping ceiling](/GSPICE/concepts/glossary/#term-clipping-ceiling)

$$
\sqrt{\frac{(N_s - 1)(N_s - N_k - 1)}{N_s}} ,
\qquad N_k = \max(\mathrm{npix} - 2\,\mathrm{nguard} - 1, 0) ,
$$

in a spectrum of typical leverage. With a constant centre, the ceiling is
$\sqrt{N_s - N_k}$. No ceiling is derived for a low-rank model.

At $N_s = 2500$, npix 2400 and `nguard=20`, $N_k = 2359$, and the ceiling is
$\sqrt{2499 \times 140 / 2500} = 11.8$. A pass at 20 then flags no pixel. `fit` logs a
warning on the `gspice` logger for each pass whose threshold exceeds the ceiling.
[A pass above the clipping ceiling](/GSPICE/guides/troubleshooting/#a-pass-above-the-clipping-ceiling)
gives the remedies.

## Constant centre

A default model subtracts the mean of its training spectra. `center=c` subtracts a
constant $c$ in flux units instead, such as a continuum level:
$x_s = a_s (\tilde f_s - c)$. The model then estimates no mean. With unit weights, the
covariance is $\sum_s x_s x_s^\mathsf{T} / N_s$, and the model needs
$N_s \ge \mathrm{npix}$.

## S/N bins

In scaled units, the noise of every spectrum has a variance near 1. The variation
between the spectra scales with $a_s^2$, so it grows with the S/N:

<SnrFigure />

Spectra of different S/N therefore have different covariances in scaled units, and one
covariance of all of them is a mixture. `snr_bins` fits one model for each
[S/N bin](/GSPICE/concepts/glossary/#term-S-N-bin).

The [S/N](/GSPICE/concepts/glossary/#term-S-N) of a spectrum is the median of
$f \sqrt{\mathrm{ivar}}$ over its good pixels
([`gspice.snr()`](/GSPICE/reference/api/#gspice.snr)). `snr_bins=n` puts the edges
between the bins at quantiles of the S/N, so that each bin holds about the same number
of spectra. A sequence gives the interior edges instead.

Each bin clips and fits on its own spectra. Bin $b$ of the model equals the model that
`fit` returns for the spectra of bin $b$ alone. At prediction, each spectrum goes to the
bin of its S/N, with the edges in `Model.edges`. Each bin needs the spectra of a model of
its kind, for example $N_s - 1 \ge \mathrm{npix}$ for a default bin. A small bin also has
a low clipping ceiling, so it flags fewer outliers.

## Low-rank model

A covariance of npix pixels cannot be estimated from fewer than npix + 1 spectra. A
[low-rank model](/GSPICE/concepts/glossary/#term-low-rank-model) asks for less. With
`rank=K`, the covariance is a [`gspice.LowRank`](/GSPICE/reference/api/#gspice.LowRank):

$$
C = V \Lambda V^\mathsf{T} + D ,
$$

with $(V, \Lambda)$ the $K$ leading eigenvectors and eigenvalues of the sample
covariance $S$, and the diagonal matrix $D$ with
$D_{ii} = S_{ii} - (V \Lambda V^\mathsf{T})_{ii}$. So $C$ and $S$ have the same variance at
every pixel, and the correlations between pixels come from the $K$ components alone. The
clipping passes use the same rank.

A low-rank model needs $N_s - 1 \ge K + 1$, a positive $K$-th eigenvalue and every
$D_{ii} > 0$, but no good pixel at every pixel. It suits a training set with fewer
spectra than pixels. [Other routes](/GSPICE/concepts/numerics/#other-routes) gives its
precision matrix.

## Noise-aware model

A default model is a [scaled model](/GSPICE/concepts/glossary/#term-scaled-model): its one
covariance holds both the variation between the spectra and their noise. That fails
where the noise differs from spectrum to spectrum in level or in shape. A
[noise-aware model](/GSPICE/concepts/glossary/#term-noise-aware-model) separates the two.

`noise=True` estimates $\Sigma$, the covariance of the
[noise-free flux](/GSPICE/concepts/glossary/#term-noise-free-flux), in flux units, from
the `ivar` of each pixel. It clips with a scaled model first, as above. At prediction,
spectrum $s$ has the covariance

$$
C_s = \Sigma_+ + \mathrm{diag}(\nu_s) ,
$$

with $\nu_{si} = 1/\mathrm{ivar}_{si}$ at the good pixels and $1/a_s^2$ at the bad
pixels. $\Sigma_+$ is $\Sigma$ with its negative eigenvalues set to 0, which biases it
upward. The working vector is $x_s = \tilde f_s - \mu$, in flux units, with $\mu$ the
mean of the training spectra, weighted by $a_s^2$ times the weights. `rank=K` also works here.

$\Sigma$ is unbiased element by element under four conditions:

1. The spectra are independent, with a common mean and a common signal covariance.
2. The noise has zero mean and the variance `1 / ivar`.
3. The noise is uncorrelated between pixels and with the signal.
4. The masks, weights and `ivar` do not depend on the flux.

The outlier flags of the final mask break condition 4, and so does an `ivar` computed
from the flux.

A noise-aware model has one bin and no centre. It needs two spectra good at every
pixel, and its clipping passes need the spectra of a scaled model. Each spectrum has its
own covariance, so each needs its own factorization
([Cost](/GSPICE/concepts/numerics/#cost)).

## What each model needs

| Model | Arguments | Spectra needed | Prediction | Finite-sample factor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Default | none | $N_s - 1 \ge \mathrm{npix}$ | one $P$ per bin | derived |
| Constant centre | `center=c` | $N_s \ge \mathrm{npix}$ | one $P$ per bin | 1 |
| S/N bins | `snr_bins=n` | per bin, as for its kind | one $P$ per bin | derived for default bins |
| Low rank | `rank=K` | $N_s - 1 \ge K + 1$ | one $P$ per bin | 1 |
| Noise-aware | `noise=True` | two good spectra at each pixel, and those of a scaled model for clipping | one factorization per spectrum | 1 |

$P$ is the [precision matrix](/GSPICE/concepts/glossary/#term-precision-matrix) of
[Numerics](/GSPICE/concepts/numerics/). A weighted model has no finite-sample factor
either ([Conditions](/GSPICE/concepts/held-out/#conditions)).
[Choose a model](/GSPICE/guides/models/) compares the models on simulated spectra.
